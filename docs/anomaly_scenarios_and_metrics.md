# Smart Meter Monitoring: Anomaly Scenarios & Metrics

## 1. Anomaly Injection Scenarios (Theoretical Definitions)
To train and evaluate the AI model effectively, we need to simulate realistic anomaly cases in our clean dataset. We will focus on two primary types of anomalies for Day 2 script development:

### Scenario 1: Energy Theft via Meter Bypass (Zero-Drop Anomaly)
**Concept:** A malicious actor attempts to bypass the meter entirely, or the meter's sensing module fails, resulting in sudden zero readings despite actual ongoing energy consumption.
**Injection Logic (Day 2):** 
1. Randomly select a subset of meters (`LCLid`).
2. For each selected meter, identify a contiguous time window (e.g., 24 to 72 hours). 
3. Replace the `KWH/hh (per half hour)` values in this window with `0.0`. 
**Real-World Context:** Represents complete physical bypass or severe hardware failure.

### Scenario 2: Calibration Tampering / Partial Bypass (Percentage-Drop Anomaly)
**Concept:** The meter is tampered with to record only a fraction of the actual energy consumed (e.g., altering internal calibration or bypassing one phase in a multi-phase system).
**Injection Logic (Day 2):**
1. Select a subset of meters.
2. Over a longer, sustained time window (e.g., 1 to 2 weeks), multiply the original `KWH/hh` values by a scaling factor (e.g., between `0.2` and `0.6`).
3. This reduces recorded consumption by 40% to 80% while retaining the natural daily shape/seasonality.
**Real-World Context:** Represents sophisticated tampering aimed at avoiding detection by maintaining a plausible, but significantly lowered, consumption profile.

## 2. Mathematical Formulas for Business Metrics

### Revenue-at-Risk (JOD)
To quantify the financial impact of a suspected anomaly and justify field team dispatch, we calculate the expected lost revenue in Jordanian Dinars (JOD).

**Formula:**
$$ \text{Revenue-at-Risk (JOD)} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \max(0, \text{Expected\_kWh}_t - \text{Actual\_kWh}_t) \times \text{Tariff\_Rate} $$

**Where:**
- $t$: Each half-hour time interval during the suspected anomaly.
- $T$: Total duration of the anomalous period.
- $\text{Expected\_kWh}_t$: The predicted baseline consumption (to be generated by our ML baseline model or historical averages).
- $\text{Actual\_kWh}_t$: The recorded anomalous consumption from the meter.
- $\text{Tariff\_Rate}$: The electricity tariff in JOD per kWh (e.g., an assumed flat rate of `0.12 JOD/kWh` for standard domestic use, though it can be adapted based on the `stdorToU` flag).

### Priority Triage Scoring
Field inspections are resource-intensive. Suspected anomalies must be prioritized using a Triage Score that balances the AI model's confidence with the financial impact.

**Primary Formula (Multiplicative Expected Value):**
$$ \text{Triage\_Score} = \text{Anomaly\_Probability} \times \text{Revenue-at-Risk (JOD)} $$
*This approach directly ranks cases by the statistical expected value of recovered revenue.*

**Alternative Formula (Weighted Normalized Score):**
If business rules dictate that duration or specific customer profiles matter as much as direct financial loss, a weighted sum can be used:
$$ \text{Triage\_Score} = (W_1 \times P_{\text{anomaly}}) + (W_2 \times R_{\text{norm}}) + (W_3 \times D_{\text{norm}}) $$

**Where:**
- $P_{\text{anomaly}}$: The confidence/probability score output by the ML model (0.0 to 1.0).
- $R_{\text{norm}}$: Normalized Revenue-at-Risk (scaled 0 to 1 relative to the current batch of alerts).
- $D_{\text{norm}}$: Normalized duration of the anomaly (longer persistence = higher urgency).
- $W_1, W_2, W_3$: Business-defined weights (e.g., $0.5, 0.4, 0.1$), summing to $1.0$.
